IFF Wristband Wiring

Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 with a Feather 32u4 RFM69 900 MHz radio (three wires: serial TX, RX and ground), the blue LED button, a Grove buzzer and a coin vibration motor. Pin names are the D labels printed on the XIAO. Unplug USB while you rewire.

3V3 power (green lead) ground (yellow lead) serial link to the Feather control signals

3V3 / ⏚ symbols = the breadboard's 3V3 and GND rails. Team convention: green lead = + (3V3), yellow lead = − (GND).

Never wire the motor straight to D5

A coin motor pulls 60 to 100 mA when it starts. A XIAO pin can safely give about 20 mA. The transistor does the switching, and the diode soaks up the spike when the motor stops. The firmware keeps the motor off until you change PIN_MOTOR to D5 in firmware/include/config.h.

RFM69 = FSK, not LoRa

The RFM69 has no LoRa mode. This build speaks FSK with channel hopping, so only an FSK node can hear it. Flash the Feather with pio run -e feather_radio -t upload (its own USB), the XIAO with pio run -e band_xiao_feather -t upload, and the laptop dongle with pio run -e dongle_fsk -t upload on an ESP32 + RFM95W. The LoRa dongle and the dog's Feather cannot hear it. Not yet tested on air.

Other radios: a loose RFM69HCW or RFM9x breakout wires straight to the XIAO instead (SCK D7, MISO D8, MOSI D9, CS/NSS D10, RST D0, DIO0 D6; see firmware/README.md). Builds: band_xiao (RFM69) and band_xiao_lora (RFM9x).

Wire colors

Team leads: green = + (3V3), yellow = − (GND). Modules bring their own colors: the Grove buzzer is red VCC, black GND and yellow SIG, and the motor + is red. Go by the pin name, not the color, and check 3V3 and GND before you plug in USB.

Parts you need
besides the modules

  • 1 × 100 Ω resistor (button LED)
  • 1 × 1 kΩ resistor (transistor base)
  • 1 × NPN transistor: 2N2222, BC337 or S8050
  • 1 × diode: 1N4148 or 1N4001
  • 78 mm of solid-core wire for the antenna
  • Jumper wires and a breadboard

If your button has 3 wires

Some LED buttons come as a small module with VCC, GND and SIG. Then wire SIG to D3, VCC to 3V3 and GND to GND, and skip the 100 Ω resistor. Tell Claude, so it sets BUTTON_MODULE 1.

Check it after wiring

  1. Plug in USB. The XIAO's LED flashes and the buzzer chirps.
  2. The boot log says #HELLO band radio=ok. radio=missing means the Feather is not answering: check it has power, TX and RX are crossed, and the grounds are joined. Send #R to ask it again.
  3. Press the button: the log shows #BTN down once, and nothing when you leave it alone.
  4. Send #C: the code 101 plays on the LEDs and buzzer (and motor, once enabled).

Later: battery

The XIAO has BAT+ and BAT− pads underneath for a 3.7 V single-cell LiPo. It charges from USB-C. Mind the polarity: a reversed battery kills the board.
